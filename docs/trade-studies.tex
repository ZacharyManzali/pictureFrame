Not stated in the briefs: connector and total heights, power draw, USB output current budget, throttling behaviour. These remain \textbf{unverified}.

\paragraph{Assessment.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Compute Modules:} rejected. They need a carrier board, which conflicts with using off-the-shelf parts and adds cost.
  \item \textbf{Zero 2 W:} the single micro-USB port must serve the drive (adapter or hub needed), Wi-Fi is 2.4 GHz only, and 512 MB of RAM is tight for SMB, web upload, email fetch and slideshow together. It has no DSI. It remains a fallback only if a prototype memory test passes.
  \item \textbf{Pi 4 B:} full-size USB ports for the drive, dual-band Wi-Fi, up to 8 GB RAM, and a smaller 3 A supply than the Pi 5. Operating range 0--50\,\textdegree C is fine for indoor use.
  \item \textbf{Pi 5:} fastest, but it asks for a 5 V/5 A supply, which makes the shared power supply larger and warmer, and the extra speed is not needed for a photo slideshow.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Decision (owner, 2026-11-04):} Raspberry Pi 4 Model B, 4 GB. The RAM size is my judgment for headroom and is not a measured requirement. Re-check against R-MEC-1 once heights are known.

\section{Storage}
Drives R-STO-1, R-STO-2, R-PWR-2, R-MEC-1.

\paragraph{Pi 4 USB limits (Raspberry Pi Pi~4 datasheet RP-008341-DS, sections 4.1 and 5.3; official power-supply documentation).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Downstream USB current is limited to about 1.1~A in aggregate over the four sockets (datasheet). The documentation table says 1.2~A; plan for 1.1~A.
  \item The Pi~4 needs a 5~V/3~A supply. A 2.5~A supply is allowed only if downstream USB devices draw under 500~mA, so a bus-powered SSD points to the 3~A supply.
  \item Low-voltage detection triggers below 4.63~V and logs a kernel entry.
  \item No official statement on UAS quirks or bus-powered SSD support was found; the quirk workaround (\texttt{usb-storage.quirks} in \texttt{cmdline.txt}) comes from forum posts only.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Candidates.} Prices are tracker snapshots and volatile; confirm before ordering. Only the 870~EVO has a datasheet power figure.

\paragraph{Assessment.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{HDD:} rejected. Spin-up surges are the worst case against a 1.1~A shared budget.
  \item \textbf{NVMe in an enclosure:} rejected. Highest peak-draw risk, highest total cost, and the enclosure may be discontinued.
  \item \textbf{SanDisk Extreme V2:} one forum thread reports disconnects on a Pi~4 (power may be the cause). Not preferred.
  \item \textbf{T7:} slim and cheaper, but its current draw is unverified. Kept as the alternative.
  \item \textbf{SATA SSD in a USB-A enclosure:} lowest risk. 2.5~W average is inside the budget (about 5.5~W at 1.1~A and 5~V), and the USB-A plug goes straight into the Pi. It is the thickest option, which no longer matters under the 60~mm depth limit.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Decision (owner, 2026-11-04):} lead with a 1~TB 2.5'' SATA SSD in a USB-A enclosure. The exact SSD and enclosure are not chosen yet. The 870~EVO with the UGREEN enclosure is the reference candidate; the 1~TB price, the enclosure's price and size, and the 870~EVO's maximum power are \textbf{unverified}.

\paragraph{Bench checks.} Run \texttt{vcgencmd get\_throttled} and watch \texttt{dmesg} for undervoltage and UAS errors with the drive and the panel running together; get the bridge's USB ID with \texttt{lsusb} before adding any quirk.

\section{Sensors}
Drives R-FTR-3, R-FTR-4. [DS] marks a datasheet or official value; [SC] marks a seller claim.

\paragraph{Pi 4 GPIO facts.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item GPIO logic is 3.3~V: VIL max 0.8~V, VIH min 2.0~V (Pi~4 datasheet RP-008341-DS, Table~3) [DS].
  \item The official documentation says GPIO pins are not 5~V tolerant and 5~V must never be applied to one [DS].
  \item The datasheet gives no absolute-maximum GPIO input voltage. Drive-current figures disagree between the datasheet and the documentation; a PIR output draws microamps, so this does not matter here.
  \item Limits on the 5~V and 3.3~V header pins were \textbf{not found} in either official document: \textbf{unverified}.
\end{itemize}
